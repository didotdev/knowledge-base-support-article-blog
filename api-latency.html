How we reduced API response latency by 42%

May 12, 2026 8 min read By Engineering Team

A practical breakdown of caching, queue prioritization, and observability improvements.

We saw the first clear signal during a burst in request volume: response times were climbing even though compute usage remained steady.

The bottleneck was not CPU; it was how our services handled repeated reads, cold caches, and slow downstream lookups.

Before changing the stack, we traced the request flow end-to-end and mapped latency across the edge, application, and dependency layers.

The biggest delay was not in business logic. It was in repeated data fetches to a high-churn dependency with uneven response times.

We introduced a short-lived cache for hot paths that do not change often, especially for customer profile lookups and settings.

This reduced duplicate reads without stale data issues because the invalidation window was tied to payload change events rather than a blanket timeout.

We also adjusted queue scheduling so customer-facing events were processed first and moved internal batch work to lower-priority workers.

Once the fixes were live, we added trace breakdowns by route, cache hit rate, and downstream dependency time to verify the improvements held under load.

The result was a 42% improvement in P95 latency during the busiest traffic window.
