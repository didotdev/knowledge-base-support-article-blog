How we reduced API response latency by 42%

May 12, 2026 8 min read By Team Engineering

We saw the first clear signal during a burst in request volume: response times were climbing even though compute usage remained steady. The bottleneck was not CPU; it was how our services were handling repeated reads, cold caches, and slow downstream lookups.

1. Identify the real bottleneck

Before changing the stack, we traced request flow end-to-end. We mapped latency across the edge layer, our application servers, and dependency calls. The biggest delay was not in our business logic. It was in repeated data fetches to a high-churn dependency with uneven response times.

The fastest improvement was not a rewrite—it was reducing unnecessary work.

2. Add smarter caching at the right layer

We introduced a short-lived cache for hot paths that do not change often, especially for customer profile lookups and settings. This reduced duplicate reads without introducing stale data issues because the invalidation window was tied to payload change events rather than a blanket timeout.

  • Short TTL for frequently accessed, low-risk data
  • Versioned cache keys for tenant-specific payloads
  • Async invalidation after permission or settings updates

3. Prioritize queue traffic

We also adjusted queue scheduling so customer-facing events were processed first. Internal batch work was throttled and moved to lower-priority workers, which ensured our API remained responsive during spikes.

4. Improve observability and alerting

Once the fixes were live, we needed confidence that the improvements would hold. We added trace breakdowns by route, cache hit rate, and downstream dependency time. This let the team spot regressions immediately and verify when a rollout was safe.

Result: average API latency dropped from 460ms to 265ms, and P95 latency improved by 42% during the busiest 10% of traffic.
